A product audit for the Nansen team · September 2026

Nansen's data, read like a forecast

TIDE is a smart-money weather service built only on the Nansen API: 51 of its 94 operations, across all 38 chains the API lists. Building it meant reading Nansen's product and API closely. This page shows what we'd bring into app.nansen.ai, each idea with a screenshot and the exact endpoints behind it, then the API issues we hit and how to fix them.

The data is the moat

Labels, flow segments, holders, perps and funding trails are all in the API. Nothing on this page needed a second data source.

The gap is the reading layer

Nansen's pages are table-first. TIDE puts the finding in each chart's title and lets any number open to its formula and the call that produced it.

What we saw

Nansen today, from a logged-out visit

We read app.nansen.ai's home and the Token God Mode page for VIRTUAL on Base, signed out, on 24 September 2026. What we'd keep and what we'd change:

What's strong

  • Label depth. Even logged out, the buyers table names wallets as "Token Millionaire", "High Activity" or "HL Perps Perma-Bear". No other source has this.
  • One place for signal and trade. The home page pairs an AI prompt ("What's trending with Smart Money today?") with a screener that goes straight to Trade.
  • Breadth per token. Buyers & Sellers, Trades, Transfers, Balances, Positions, History and Performance, plus Holders and a PnL leaderboard.
  • Segments that mean something. Flow Intelligence splits a token's flow into smart money, exchanges, top PnL traders, public figures, whales and fresh wallets.

Where TIDE would push

  • The story is left to the reader. Most panels are tables and a candle chart. Nothing says what they add up to: is this token being accumulated or distributed?
  • Scores a reader can't take apart. The header shows Safety 88/100 and Snipers 15/100. We'd let every composite open to its inputs and formula in one click, the way every TIDE number does.
  • Placeholder values in the page text. When Flow Intelligence is locked, its text says "$1.00M" and "1000000 wallets" for every segment. Screen readers and copy-paste pick these up as real figures.
  • No view across chains. The API ranks chains (chains/chain-rank) and screens every chain. Signed out, we didn't find one place in the app that answers "which chains are gaining money right now".
Ideas to take

Seven ideas, working today

Each works across chains, runs on the endpoints listed, and never shows a number Nansen didn't return: when Nansen has nothing, the panel says so.

Reading layer

Say the finding, not the chart type

Every card title is a sentence built from the same numbers the chart draws: "Base today is mostly down: 10 of 96 most-traded tokens up". A title can't claim something the data doesn't show, and a reader who only scans titles still leaves with the day's story.

For Nansen: the same tables, with a one-line verdict on top. It's the cheapest change here, and the one that most changes how the product feels.

Trust

Show the math behind every number

Every figure in TIDE has an ⓘ that opens its formula, the inputs as they were read, and the exact Nansen request: endpoint, body, credit cost, and whether it was served live or from cache. Every token page carries a receipt, such as "This page: 69 Nansen calls, 23 credits (54 from cache)".

For Nansen: analysts share screenshots. A number that shows its source is a number people repeat, with Nansen's name on it.

Cross-chain

A weather map for 38 chains

The Chain Pressure Index scores each chain 0–100 on net flow against its own history, blended over 1h, 24h and 7d. A hex map groups chains by family; isobars mark chains where pressure is rising, and rotation fronts show capital moving from one chain to another. Chains Nansen doesn't cover say "no reading" rather than showing a neutral 50.

For Nansen: a landing view that answers "where is money going today" before anyone picks a token.

Perps

Where forced selling would happen

For any token with a Hyperliquid perp, TIDE sums the 100 largest open positions into bands by distance from the mark. Longs liquidate below the mark (forced sells), shorts above it (forced buys). The densest band is outlined, and the largest positions are listed with their leverage.

For Nansen: Token God Mode already lists positions. This adds what those positions mean for price, in one glance.

Holders

Holders as a constellation

The top 90 holders sit on a 3D sphere, sized by share of supply. They're linked only where Nansen shows a connection: a shared first funder, related wallets, or a transfer between two holders today. Linked wallets pull into clusters you can rotate toward; hover a wallet to isolate its links. Exchanges and contracts are greyed out and not counted as holders.

For Nansen: a holder table can't show that nine of the top 25 wallets were funded from the same place. This picture does.

Risk

A dump-risk score you can audit, with odds that keep score

The Storm Score combines six inputs Nansen already serves: holder concentration, insider clusters, wind shear between wallet segments, exit liquidity, sell pressure, and Nansen's own risk indicators. Each input is shown, and missing inputs are counted in a confidence figure instead of being filled in. Beside it, calibrated 7-day odds of a 50% fall or a 30% rise, each with its out-of-sample track record: AUC, the number of tokens, and the test date.

For Nansen: "Safety 88/100" becomes a score people can argue with, and a forecast that reports its own hit rate.

Chain pages

Rank a chain against every chain

Each chain page opens with its pressure ring and where it ranks by DEX volume, active addresses and transactions. The ranking panel orders its tabs by the chain's best placing, so each chain opens on its strongest number, not its weakest: Base opens on "#4 of 24 by DEX volume".

For Nansen: chains/chain-rank already answers "is this chain growing", one credit per call. Nobody sees that answer today.

A public view that follows the rules

Nansen's redistribution classes are enforced in code. Public pages strip every label, withhold smart-money-filtered data, and explain what's withheld and why. A browser test crawls the public site and fails if any real label appears, including in React's serialized page data.

For the API team

Nine things we hit in the API

Each was found in live calls, and TIDE works around each one in code. Most are small doc or schema fixes.

WhereWhat happensSuggested fix
x-nansen-credits-cost on 4xx
cost clarity		A validation error carries the endpoint's price in the cost header, but the balance doesn't change.Send 0 on uncharged errors, or document that 4xx is never billed. We had to confirm it by checking the balance.
ra-agent/posts-by-token
cost clarity		Without token_symbol it returns 200 with no posts and still costs 5 credits.Make token_symbol required (422), like the other token endpoints.
tgm/position-intelligence
request shape		Rejects chain (422 unknown_field). The token_address field takes the Hyperliquid perp symbol, e.g. AERO.Rename it token_symbol, as tgm/perp-positions already does.
profiler/address/pnl
schema vs live		Live calls require a date range. The schema marks it optional.Mark it required, or apply a default window.
v1beta1/profiler/historical-transaction-lookup
request shape		Looking up a transaction by hash times out unless block_timestamp is also sent.Document block_timestamp as effectively required, or index lookups by hash.
transaction-with-token-transfer-lookup
schema vs live		Returns nft_transfer_array: null where the schema promises an array.Return [], or mark the field nullable.
tgm/jup-dca
response shape		Input and output mint addresses end in a NUL character (\u0000), so comparing mints fails.Trim the mints on the server.
profiler/dex-trades
schema vs live		Rejects chain: "all", so a wallet's DEX trades take one call per chain.Accept all, or list the allowed chains in this endpoint's enum.
13 operations without a schema
no schema		account, ra-agent/*, search/web-search, search/web-fetch and the perp trading endpoints appear in the docs without a published schema. We found their request shapes through 422 messages.Publish them in the OpenAPI file. The 422 messages are already clear enough to generate schemas from.
