# 02 · Presentation strategy

## Core message

**"Nansen's data, turned into answers."** Peregrine does not show more tables; it answers the trader's questions (where is money going, is this token safe, is this wallet good) and can explain any number on screen.

## What the viewer should feel

Calm confidence. "This is a serious, finished product that knows the market." Not "a dashboard with many pages". The film should feel fast but never frantic: each shot lands one idea.

## What to emphasise

1. **Answers, not data:** plain-English headlines ("Accumulating Ton.", "Rug risk: High"), big verdicts and scores.
2. **Depth behind the answer:** the Token Score rings split into Nansen risk and Peregrine's model; the market map of 150 tokens; the Proof page's 7,903 calls.
3. **Ask anything:** the AI layer grounded in what is on screen.
4. **It is an app, not a site:** the phone experience with Liquid Glass and Genie Ask.

## Communicating depth without overwhelming

- One number per shot carries the meaning; everything else is texture. Overlays name the idea in 2–4 words ("Where money is moving", "Is it safe?"), never explain a formula.
- Push the camera in on the one element that matters (a ring, a verdict, a count), then let the rest of the screen blur by scale, not by effect.
- Show the count of things once (82 endpoints, 7,903 calls) on the Proof shot instead of listing features.

## Balancing function and polish

Every polish moment is also a function moment: the flow ring animates *because* it shows money moving; the genie opens *because* the user asks a question; the glass lens glides *because* the user changes tab. No pure design-demo shots.

## Making Nansen obvious

- The token page's Token Score ring legend includes "Nansen risk": frame it.
- The rug page shows "Nansen risk indicators" and "Rug risk" in Nansen's data: readable in frame.
- The Proof shot shows call counts by Nansen endpoint family.
- The end card: Nansen logo, "Powered by Nansen".
- The "Analyze with Nansen" button name is readable when the AI moment starts.

## Making it feel novel

Lead with things other dashboards do not have: a sentence answer on the home screen, a score that is half Nansen and half a backtested model, an AI panel that answers about a picked chart, and a native-feeling phone app with a genie animation.

## Screen time

| Weight | Features |
|---|---|
| Most | Overview hero, token page (score + chart), Analyze / Genie Ask, phone app |
| Medium | Alpha market map, rug verdict, Proof |
| Glimpse | Chain flows (Flow Index lines), Profiler trader score, predictions and perps boards, Token Checker table |

## Narrative order (why this order)

Macro → micro → judgement → explanation → portability → trust:
market read (Overview) → what is moving (Alpha map, Flow Index) → pick one token (token page) → is it safe (rug verdict) → ask why (Analyze) → who holds it and are they good (Profiler glimpse) → take it with you (phone, Genie Ask, glass) → why trust it (Proof) → Powered by Nansen.

## Desktop and mobile

Desktop carries depth (dense charts, the market map, the token page). Mobile carries delight and motion (glass, genie, gestures). The switch happens on a question: the desktop AI answer resolves, then the same question gesture continues on the phone, so the device change feels like one workflow moving to the pocket.

## Fast but understandable

- 1.5–4 s per shot; nothing static longer than 1 s (always a pan, push or live animation).
- Hard cuts on the beat within a scene; one designed transition between acts.
- Text overlays: at most 4 words, on screen ≥ 1.2 s, bottom-left safe area, never over the data being shown.
